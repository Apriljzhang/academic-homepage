# 期刊論文同行評審

Dr April Jiawei Zhang · City University of Macau  
90-minute workshop · Prepared 18 November 2026

## Purpose and outcomes

This session develops participants’ ability to evaluate journal submissions in education and applied linguistics and communicate defensible, constructive judgements. By the end, participants should be able to:

1. Decide whether to accept an invitation, considering expertise, availability and conflicts.
2. Evaluate the relationship between research questions, methods, evidence and claims.
3. Distinguish missing reporting, remediable weaknesses and design limitations.
4. Write prioritised comments that explain the problem, its consequence and the required revision.
5. Justify a recommendation and assess whether an author’s response resolves a concern.

本課程旨在培養參與者評估教育及應用語言學期刊稿件的能力，並以有理據、具建設性的方式溝通判斷。完成後應能判斷是否接受審稿邀請、評估問題—方法—證據—主張的一致性、區分資訊缺失與設計限制、撰寫有優次的修訂要求，以及解釋處理建議並檢查作者回應。

## Preparation

- Participants: postgraduate researchers or early-career academics with basic experience reading empirical papers.
- Materials: the online lesson, this pack, and one public review exchange from Language Testing’s reviewer-resources page.
- Grouping: pairs for annotation and calibration; individual report drafting.
- Open the external resources before teaching. The journal’s resource page and its three exemplar links were verified; supplementary PDF contents were not incorporated into this pack. Availability may depend on the publisher’s access controls. Use the fictional re-review exchange below if the PDFs cannot be opened.
- Use public materials, an explicitly fictional teaching case or manuscripts whose use for training has been authorised. A reviewer’s own access to a submission does not authorise classroom distribution.
- The English/Traditional Chinese toggle translates the current slide. Entries in equivalent response fields are shared across both languages. Practice responses are stored only in the browser, if available; participants can download them as text. A lesson printout uses the currently selected language and includes model answers.

Timing is a facilitator guide, not a prescribed journal workflow. For a 60-minute version, assign the public-review annotation as pre-work and shorten whole-group calibration; retain individual report writing. For a two-hour session, add a second case from a different methodological tradition and a longer author–reviewer role-play.

## Activity 2: Comment-writing clinic

Weak comment: “The methodology is weak and the analysis is not rigorous. Please improve it.”

Practice detail: A survey measures willingness to use AI, but the conclusion describes actual uptake. No usage records were collected.

Write 50–80 English words, or approximately 100–160 Chinese characters, locating the mismatch, explaining its consequence and requiring a feasible revision.

Possible answer: In the Discussion, the conclusion that students frequently used AI is based on a willingness scale, not observed use. This conflates intention with behaviour. Please revise the outcome description throughout the manuscript to willingness to use AI, explain this measurement limitation, and remove claims about actual uptake unless independent usage evidence is available.

可參考：討論部分以使用意願量表支持學生經常使用AI的結論，混淆了意圖與行為。請把全文的結果描述修訂為使用AI的意願，交代測量限制；如沒有獨立的使用證據，應刪除有關實際使用的主張。

## Activity 3: Fictional manuscript

**All details and numbers below are invented for teaching. This is not an actual submission or published study.**

**Title:** AI feedback improves EFL writing and motivation in Macau undergraduates.

**§1 Aim.** The study asks whether AI feedback improves writing and motivation more than teacher feedback over six weeks.

**§2 Method.** Two intact first-year classes at one university participate: Class A (n = 30) uses AI feedback with Teacher A; Class B (n = 30) receives teacher feedback from Teacher B. There is one class per condition, no random assignment, and a shared syllabus. Time spent on feedback is not recorded. The same writing task is used before and after the intervention. Each teacher scores their own class using a 20-point rubric. Rater training, blinding and agreement are not reported. Institutional ethics approval and voluntary consent are stated; participation does not affect grades.

**§3 Results.**

| Table 1 | AI class | Teacher-feedback class |
|---|---:|---:|
| Enrolled | 30 | 30 |
| Complete writing pairs | 24 | 29 |
| Pre-test mean (SD), completers | 10.0 (2.0) | 12.0 (2.0) |
| Post-test mean (SD), completers | 13.0 (2.0) | 14.0 (2.0) |
| Mean within-person gain | 3.0 | 2.0 |

Separate paired t tests are reported as p < .01 in each class. There is no direct comparison of change, no effect size or confidence interval, and no explanation of incomplete pairs. The authors infer a stronger AI effect from the larger raw gain. Change-score SDs and pre–post correlations are not available.

**§4 Motivation.** Only at post-test, 24 AI-class students rate “The tool was useful”, “I liked the feedback” and “I would use it again”; the mean is 4.4/5. There is no baseline or comparison-class measurement.

**§5 Interviews.** Six AI-class volunteers are interviewed in Chinese by Teacher A. The paper calls its analysis “thematic analysis”, provides two positive quotations in English, and does not explain coding, theme development or translation.

**§6 Conclusion.** “AI feedback caused superior writing gains, increased motivation and should replace teacher feedback in universities.”

### Drafting task

Prepare a report with a short overview, three prioritised major comments, warranted minor comments and a recommendation to the editor. The three-comment target is for practice; a real review should cover all material concerns. Do not invent missing information or imply misconduct from omitted reporting.

### Facilitator answer guide

A strong review should recognise these linked concerns:

1. **Confounding:** class, teacher and treatment coincide. There are no independent class replications. A participant-level adjusted model cannot identify the modality effect independently of teacher and class. Baseline adjustment, if explored, does not cure the design.
2. **Analysis and attrition:** two within-class tests do not test the between-class difference in change. There are six incomplete pairs in the AI class and one in the comparison class. Ask for reasons, treatment of missingness and an appropriately limited analysis. The supplied marginal SDs cannot determine the uncertainty of a gain contrast without further information.
3. **Score interpretation:** teachers score their own classes; comparable scoring has not been established. Repeated use of the same task introduces a possible practice explanation. Clarify what was done and acknowledge residual limitations; do not assert that training or blinding definitely never occurred.
4. **Construct mismatch:** usefulness, liking and future intention measured after the intervention do not establish a change in a specified motivation construct. The central correction is to narrow the claims, not merely relabel the scale as validated.
5. **Qualitative transparency:** the teacher–student relationship, volunteer sampling, analytic procedure and translation require explanation. Two favourable quotations alone do not demonstrate the basis for the reported interpretations. Do not automatically demand inter-rater agreement across every qualitative approach.
6. **Overextended implications:** replacing teacher feedback across universities exceeds the evidence from two classes at one university.

The model comments on lesson slides 22–23 are examples, not the only acceptable answer. Participants may group related concerns differently. A review that identifies only p values and grammar misses the central inference problem.

### Sample overall assessment and recommendation

This classroom comparison addresses a relevant question about AI-supported feedback and includes repeated writing assessments. Its potential contribution is a contextual account of two classes’ experiences. The present design and outcome measures do not establish causal superiority or increased motivation.

**Recommendation:** Reject if a causal effectiveness claim is necessary to the article’s contribution or to the journal’s threshold. Major revision may be defensible if the journal considers exploratory classroom accounts and the existing data can support substantially narrower claims, clearer scoring and analytic reporting, and a transparent treatment of the interviews. Explain which possibility underlies the recommendation. Do not present a statistical adjustment as a cure for class/teacher confounding.

There is no compulsory single recommendation for this exercise: assess the reasoning and the feasibility of the proposed route. Acceptance or minor revision would require a persuasive explanation of how the central unresolved problems are already addressed; the supplied case offers no such evidence.

## Peer feedback rubric

This is an original formative teaching rubric, not a validated instrument or journal acceptance score. Use “not yet clear / partly clear / clear” for each dimension and identify a concrete revision.

| Dimension | Evidence of an effective review |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | Represents the study and claims correctly; distinguishes reported facts from inference. |
| Evidence | Locates each substantive concern in the manuscript. |
| Reasoning | Explains the consequence for interpretation, integrity or contribution. |
| Actionability | Requests feasible and proportionate changes; allows justified alternatives. |
| Prioritisation | Leads with central concerns and groups related issues coherently. |
| Fairness | Critiques the work respectfully and applies criteria suited to the approach. |
| Decision coherence | Recommendation follows from the concerns and the possibility of revision. |

**Partner feedback:** One effective comment; one comment requiring revision; the change I made.

## Re-review exercise and fallback exchange

Reviewer: “Remove the claim that AI caused superior gains.”  
Author response: “Thank you. We have added this limitation.”  
Revised conclusion: “AI feedback is more effective than teacher feedback.”

**Question:** Has the concern been resolved? What change is still required?

**Suggested follow-up:** The added limitation acknowledges the concern, but the comparative effectiveness claim remains in the Conclusion. Please revise that claim and align the abstract and discussion with the exploratory design.

A response letter is not evidence that the manuscript has been changed appropriately. A well-supported disagreement can be acceptable; check whether the concern is addressed, not whether the author simply complies. Explain substantive new concerns if a revision creates or reveals them.
