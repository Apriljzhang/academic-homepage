Formative assessment / AfL

Elicit and interpret evidence of learning, then use it to decide how teaching and learning should proceed towards a clear goal. Plan fresh evidence to check progress.

Opening case: a learner chooses the better-supported paragraph, “B, because it is longer.” What does the reason suggest? What else might explain it? Compare equally long relevant and irrelevant examples, discuss the difference, then ask for a new justification.

How the five strategies appear in our language activity
  1. Clarifying, sharing and understanding learning intentions. Make the language focus and two success criteria visible and understood.
  2. Engineering effective discussions, tasks, and activities that elicit evidence of learning. Ask learners to choose, justify and write so the reasoning becomes visible.
  3. Providing feedback that moves learners forward. Offer a checked hint that enables a purposeful next step.
  4. Activating students as learning resources for one another. Test a partner’s activity and discuss what the responses show.
  5. Activating students as owners of their own learning. Revise, explain the change and inspect fresh evidence.

Black & Wiliam (2009).

Our design: keep the language goal central while eliciting evidence, interpreting it, providing feedback and supporting learner/teacher action. Evidence unclear → investigate; gap evident → support; goal met → move on or extend. A fresh check can occur in a later lesson.

Evidence and Explanation Activity: paper version

Suppose intermediate English learners state opinions but struggle to support them. Goal: support an opinion with a specific relevant example and explain its link.

  1. A specific example is relevant to the claim.
  2. An explanation shows how the example supports the claim.

Claim: Students should have a short reading period every school day.

Sample A

Reading every day is good. It is useful and important. Students will become better because reading helps them.

Sample B

A short reading period can help students build a reading habit. For example, learners could finish one short article each day and record two unfamiliar words. This regular practice would give them repeated opportunities to meet vocabulary in context.

Choose a sample and justify it with the criteria. Identify its example and explanation.

Improve A: write your first attempt before reading the hints.

Hints: cover until you have attempted the task

Can the reader picture your example? Explain how it supports the claim. In B, the daily article and unfamiliar words give an example; “This regular practice…” explains a possible link to vocabulary exposure. Length alone does not show support.

Revise, keeping your first writing above. Explain what changed and why.

Fresh recheck: support “Students should sometimes choose what they read” with a new example and explanation.
