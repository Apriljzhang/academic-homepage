Evidence and Explanation Lab

Support an opinion with a specific relevant example and explain its link.

Two success criteria
  1. The example is specific and relevant to the claim.
  2. The explanation shows how the example supports the claim.

Claim: Students should have a short reading period every school day.

Sample A

Reading every day is good. It is useful and important. Students will become better because reading helps them.

Sample B

A short reading period can help students build a reading habit. For example, learners could finish one short article each day and record two unfamiliar words. This regular practice would give them repeated opportunities to meet vocabulary in context.

Feedback: check the evidence

Sample B provides a daily article and two unfamiliar words as a specific example. “This regular practice…” explains a possible link to vocabulary exposure. A longer response is not automatically a better response, and agreement is not evidence.

  1. Example: Can a reader picture what learners would actually do during the reading period?
  2. Link: Add a sentence explaining why that example could help. A “because” link can help, but the word alone is not evidence.
